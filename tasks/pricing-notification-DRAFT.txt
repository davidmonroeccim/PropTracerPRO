Hi [FIRST NAME],

PropTracerPRO pricing changes on [DATE]. Some of it goes up and some
of it comes down, and I'd rather walk you through it than have you
piece it together from an invoice.

Here's what it looks like.

If you're on Pro or you're an AcquisitionPRO member:

   You already have the owner's name       $0.15 per successful trace
   You don't, or you want the full
   property record                         $0.25 per record

If you're pay as you go:

   You already have the owner's name       $0.25 per successful trace
   You don't, or you want the full
   property record                         $0.40 per record

It makes no difference whether the owner turns out to be a person or
an LLC. Same price either way.

The $97 Pro subscription isn't changing.

What that means in practice. If you're on Pro and you work
company-owned property where you already have the owner name, you'll
pay less than you do today, because that trace drops from $0.25 to
$0.15. On pay as you go that same trace stays where it is. Most of the
rest goes up.

Why it changed at all. Finding the owner of a commercial property is a
different problem than finding the owner of a house. The method we had
been leaning on worked reasonably well on property held by a person
and fell down on property held by an LLC. Most commercial property is
held by an LLC. So we rebuilt that part of the product on county
assessor records, which is where the owner of record actually lives.
That data costs us real money on every lookup, and the new pricing is
what it takes to run it.

Now the part worth reading, because we've never charged for it before
and we've never explained it either.

When you send us a record without an owner name, you don't just get a
name back anymore. You get the property record behind it, better than
60 fields of it.

Every one carries the parcel number, the lot size, the assessed value,
the land use, map coordinates, and whether the owner lives somewhere
other than the property. Most carry the building size, the last sale
date, and how long the current owner has held it. A good number carry
the year built, the open mortgage balance, the lender and the flood
zone. Fewer carry the unit count, the last sale price, and how much
other property that same owner holds.

Which ones land depends on the county. Some counties publish a lot and
some publish very little, so the record runs deeper in some markets
than it does in others. You are buying the county's record, not a
guess at what the county might have said.

That's the reason the higher tier costs what it costs. You're buying
the property file, not just a phone number.

One thing I want to be straight about, because it's a change and I
don't want you finding it on a statement. The lower tier works the way
it always has. You pay when we find somebody, and a miss costs you
nothing. The higher tier is priced per record you send, whether or not
contacts come back, because we're buying that property record either
way.

A couple of honest limits while I'm at it. Assessed value is the
county's assessed value. It is not a market value and we don't
pretend it is. And the sale and mortgage fields are only there when
the county publishes them, which is most of the time but not all of
it.

If this creates a problem for how you're using PropTracerPRO, hit
reply and tell me. I read these myself.

David

1. TIMING, NOW HISTORICAL. This notice describes both tiers as live.
   When it was written the per-record tier was not wired. That is
   still true at the time of this note; it is the push gate, not a
   mailing gate.

2. THE CHANGE IS MIXED, NOT A FLAT INCREASE. Verified against main,
   where entity rows bill a flat $0.25 through
   CHARGE_PER_FASTAPPEND_SUCCESS (lib/trace/settleBulkJob.ts:184):

   Owner known, individual, Pro       $0.07 to $0.15   up 2.1x
   Owner known, individual, PAYG      $0.11 to $0.25   up 2.3x
   Owner known, entity, Pro           $0.25 to $0.15   DOWN
   Owner known, entity, PAYG          $0.25 to $0.25   unchanged
   Owner unknown, Pro                 about $0.25 to $0.25   flat
   Owner unknown, PAYG                about $0.25 to $0.40   up

   The handoff measured 22 of 24 commercial parcels as entity-owned.
   So a Pro user who already has owner names sees the dominant
   commercial case get CHEAPER, and the same user on pay as you go
   sees it unchanged. Only the individual-owner and unknown-owner
   cases are increases.

   An earlier draft opened with "it's going up" flat. That was wrong
   and would have spent goodwill you did not need to spend.

   Under this model owner type no longer affects price at all, so the
   email says so in one line rather than showing customers a matrix.

3. WHY I WORDED THE REASON THAT WAY. I did not write that the old
   method failed outright. The evidence says it worked on individually
   owned property and failed on entity-owned property, and 22 of the
   24 commercial parcels tested were entity-owned. Saying it plainly
   failed would be inaccurate and would invite refund requests for AI
   Search charges already billed.

4. EVERY FIELD NAMED IS ONE THE HANDOFF VERIFIED. Nothing here claims
   estimated value, equity, high equity, free and clear, or any
   propensity score. The handoff shows estimated value is just the
   assessed value with no AVM behind it. The email says assessed value
   is not market value, on purpose.

5. FILL RATES ARE HEDGED, NOT PROMISED. The copy groups fields by
   their measured rate and never promises one above what was measured.
   "Every one" is the 100 percent set. "Most" is the 70 to 91 percent
   band: building size 91, year built 78, stories 70, mortgage balance
   70, lender 74. "Roughly half" is sale price at 52. "About four in
   ten" is unit count at 43.

   An earlier draft of this listed unit count and stories flat in with
   the 100 percent fields. That was an overclaim on the lowest-filling
   field in the set and it is corrected above.

6. "60+ FIELDS" IS CORRECT AND I WAS WRONG TO QUESTION IT. Counted
   directly from the 24 saved raw vendor responses: the dossier
   returns 86 fields, and 46 of them carry a usable value at least
   somewhere. Your framing was right too. Which fields are useful
   depends on the county, which is why the copy above bands them by
   how often they land instead of listing them flat.

   I had claimed the handoff only supported 30. That was me reading
   its illustrative list as an exhaustive one. The full inventory is
   now written into the handoff so nobody has to re-derive it.

   The handoff called all eleven status flags "100%". That is a
   presence count. Measured over 24 records:

   absentee_owner     TRUE on 21 of 24  (88%)
   owner_occupied     TRUE on  1 of 24  (4%)
   vacant, tax_delinquent, tax_lien, pre_foreclosure, foreclosure,
   inherited, death, judgment, hoa    TRUE on 0 of 24

   Could be that commercial property genuinely isn't distressed, could

   can't tell us. Either way, selling "tax delinquent and
   pre-foreclosure flags" would be selling something with no evidence
   behind it, so the copy now names only the absentee owner signal,
   which is real and is the useful one for CRE anyway.
